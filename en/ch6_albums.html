Chapter 6

Album management

Use albums to sort your photos into groups: create an album, add photos to it, arrange the order, archive and lock. By the end of this chapter, you'll have turned your photos from one long timeline into a structured set of memories.

Albums are the basic unit for organizing memories

The timeline is good for looking back; organizing is what albums are for. A trip, a project, your child's first birthday: each one can be an album that gathers the photos scattered across the timeline. From then on, finding photos and sharing photos happens album by album.

Chapter task: create an album, add a few photos to it, and adjust the order. Creating an album and adding photos to it changes how the photos are "grouped", but does not touch the original files.

Albums, Archive and Locked Folder: how they differ

ConceptWhat it doesBest for
AlbumsCollect photos by hand into a named setTrips, events, grouping by theme
ArchiveHide photos from the main timeline, but keep themOld photos, or ones you rarely look at but do not want to delete
Locked FolderPhotos that need a second verification before you can view themSensitive content such as ID documents and receipts
One photo can be in many albums: an album does not "move" photos, it "includes" them. The same photo can appear in several albums at once, and deleting an album does not delete the photos.

Six steps to create and organize an album

  1. Open Albums

    In the sidebar, under the Library group, click Albums. You'll see the list of albums (if it is empty, a hint is shown instead).

  2. Create a new album

    Click "Create album" or "New album" and enter a name for the album, for example "Okinawa trip 2026".

  3. Select photos to add

    After you create it, the system lets you pick photos; or, later on, select photos on the timeline and add them with "Add to album" in the menu.

  4. Open the album and check

    Open the album and confirm that all the photos are there. You can keep adding or removing photos.

  5. Adjust the order and the cover

    Depending on the version, you can sort by hand and set a cover, so the album is easier to recognize.

  6. Share when you need to

    When you want to show it to someone later, use the sharing feature in Chapter 8. Do not just hand over your account and password.

Deleting an album ≠ deleting photos: deleting an album usually only stops it from "including" the photos; the photos stay on the timeline. To actually delete photos you have to delete them through Trash, and they are held there first.

Archive and Locked Folder

Archive is for photos you want "out of sight, but not deleted". Once archived, a photo is hidden from the main timeline; you can still find it under Archive and restore it to the timeline.

Locked Folder is a more private space that asks you to verify again (for example, by entering a password) before you can open it. It suits photos such as ID documents and receipts, the kind you do not want someone next to you to see with a single swipe.

Locked ≠ encrypted: Locked Folder is protection at the interface level; it stops someone next to you from casually flipping through your photos, but it is not file encryption. For genuinely sensitive material, you still need overall account security on top (Chapter 11).

Common album pitfalls

  1. You cannot find the button to create an album

    Make sure you are on the Albums page (sidebar: Library → Albums), not the timeline. The button is usually at the top of the page or in the top right.

  2. Photos will not go into the album

    After selecting the photos, look for "Add to album" in the menu; or open the album and use "Add photos" to pick them from inside it.

  3. An album has disappeared

    Check whether it was renamed, or look for it in the Albums list in the sidebar; you can also search for the album name with the search from Chapter 7.

  4. You deleted an album and the photos vanished too

    First check whether you chose "remove from album" or "delete". Removing from the album does not affect the timeline; deleting sends the photos to Trash, where they can be restored within 30 days.

Albums FAQ

Is there a limit on how many photos an album can hold?
There is no hard limit, but cramming thousands of photos into one album slows loading. Split them into several albums by theme; that actually makes them easier to find.
Can I set a cover for an album?
Yes. Inside the album, pick a photo you like and set it as the cover; the album list becomes much easier to scan.
Do archived photos take up space?
Yes. Archiving only hides; it does not delete and does not save space. To free up space you have to delete the photos and empty Trash.
What if I forget the Locked Folder password?
The Locked Folder password is usually your account password or one you set separately. If you forget it, ask your admin for help; do not keep guessing.
Can I share an album across accounts?
Yes. Use sharing (Chapter 8) to share the album with other users, or generate a link for people outside.
