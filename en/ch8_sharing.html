Chapter 8

Share albums

Share an album safely with family and friends: generate a shared link, set permissions and an expiry, and revoke it afterwards. By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to let other people see the photos you choose without handing over your account password.

Sharing hands over a key, not a door

When you want family and friends to see your photos, the worst way to do it is to give them your account password — that hands over your whole photo library. The right way is a shared link: it opens only the one album you choose, and you can set an expiry and control whether downloads are allowed. Learn it, and sharing does not have to turn into a leak.

Chapter task: generate a shared link for one album, set its permissions, and learn how to revoke it. Sharing means "opening an album to the outside", so think through the scope before you act.

Two kinds of recipients, and the security line

RecipientMethodRisk
Other users on this serverShare the album with another account on the same Immich serverLow: they still have to log in
People outside (no account)Generate a public shared link; anyone who has the link can view itMedium: the link is a key, so keep it under control
The link is the key: once a public link gets out, anyone who has it can view the album. Before you share, make sure the album holds no photos you do not want public.

Six steps to share an album

  1. Pick the album to share

    Find the album under Albums and confirm that its contents are exactly what you want the other person to see.

  2. Open sharing

    In the album's Share menu, choose how you want to share.

  3. Choose a recipient or generate a link

    For a user on this server: pick their account. For someone outside: generate a shared link.

  4. Set permissions and an expiry

    Depending on the version, you can set whether downloads are allowed, when the link expires, whether a password is required, and so on.

  5. Send the link

    Send the link to the recipient (over a channel you trust). Remind them that it is a private link and not to forward it.

  6. Revoke it when you are done

    Once they have seen the photos, go back to the sharing settings and disable or delete the link. The key stops working.

Hard stop: do not paste a shared link into public forums, group chats or web pages. If you need a long-term public showcase, consider a more formal way of publishing instead, and assess that risk separately.

Manage and revoke shares

The Sharing section in the sidebar lists the albums you have shared and the ones other people have shared with you. Here you can:

  • See the recipient and permissions of each share.
  • Disable or delete a shared link so it stops working immediately.
  • Adjust permissions (for example, turn off "download allowed").
Make revoking a habit: a shared link is like a key you have lent out, so remember to revoke it afterwards. That is especially true for a one-off share with a vendor or an event organizer.

Common sharing pitfalls

  1. The other person says the link will not open

    Check that the link is complete and was not cut off; check that the share has not been disabled and has not expired.

  2. The other person cannot see certain photos

    Check whether those photos are in the shared album. Sharing works per "album", so anything not in the album is naturally not visible.

  3. You want them to view but not download

    Turn off the "download" permission in the sharing settings (depending on the version).

  4. You forgot what you have shared

    Take inventory in the Sharing section and disable the shares you no longer need.

Sharing FAQ

Do shared links expire?
You can set an expiration date (depending on the version). Once it passes, the link stops working automatically and you have to generate a new one. For a one-off share, we recommend setting an expiry.
Does the other person need an Immich account?
It depends on how you share. A public link needs no account; anyone with the link can view. Sharing with another user requires that they already have an account.
Can the other person delete or edit the photos I share?
Not by default. A share is normally "read-only" viewing; the other person cannot touch your original photos.
Can I revoke a share?
Yes. Disable or delete the shared link at any time; it takes effect immediately.
Where do I see the albums other people have shared with me?
In the Sharing section in the sidebar. It lists the albums other people have shared with you.
