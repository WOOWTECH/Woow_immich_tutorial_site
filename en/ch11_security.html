Chapter 11

Security and minimal exposure

Run a systematic security check, from the entry point and your account to your shares and what is public. By the end of this chapter, you'll know which habits protect your memories, which "conveniences" are really exposing too much, and how to shrink your exposure to the minimum.

Your photos are the last thing that should leak

Photos hold people, places and the details of your life. Once they leak, the damage is harder to undo than a leaked password. Security is not "set it once and you are done"; it is a few habits kept up over the long run. This chapter turns the checks into a checklist: follow it and the risk stays very low.

Chapter task: go through the entry point, your account, your shares and what is public one item at a time, and close off anything that is exposed without need. Nothing in this chapter deletes data; you only tighten.

Three lines of defense

Line of defenseWhat it protectsYour responsibility
Transport (HTTPS)Encryption of the data between you and the serverMake sure the URL is https, and stop if you see a warning
Identity (account and password)Who can log inA strong password, never reused, and sign out on shared devices
Authorization (sharing scope)What can be seen after loginShare only the albums that are needed, and revoke afterwards
The minimal-exposure principle: nothing is public by default. When something has to be public, open it only as far as "just enough", and keep the ability to revoke it.

Six-step security checklist

  1. Check that the entry point is https

    The browser's address bar must show https://your-immich.example, with the padlock icon. Note: replace this with the URL of your own Immich server (the one WoowTech gave you, or your own). If a "Not secure" warning appears, stop.

  2. Check your password strength

    Your password should be long enough, and not shared with any other service. If it has been a long time since you changed it, change it now.

  3. Take inventory of accounts

    Admins: take inventory under Users (Chapter 10). Is there an account that should have been disabled but is still active? Are there more Admins than necessary?

  4. Take inventory of shares

    Go to the Sharing section, disable the shares you no longer need, and check the permissions and expiry of every public link.

  5. Check what is public

    Make sure the admin panel and the whole photo grid are not public. If something has to be public, keep it to the albums that are actually needed.

  6. Make revoking a habit

    Revoke a one-off share as soon as it has served its purpose, and rerun this checklist regularly (monthly, for example).

If you see a certificate warning: when the browser shows "Your connection is not private", do not click "Proceed to…". It may be a sign that the network has been hijacked; contact your admin first.

Everyday security habits

  • Always sign out when you are done on a shared (public) computer, and close the browser.
  • Never paste a shared link into a public forum or group chat, and never forward it to people who have no reason to have it.
  • If you get a suspicious "reset your password" or "update" notification, verify where it came from first; do not click the link on impulse.
  • Check the storage usage in the sidebar and the Sharing list regularly, so you know where things stand.
  • Apply the same sign-out and password rules to the Immich mobile app.
Security is a habit, not an event: rather than waiting for something to go wrong and patching it up afterwards, turn this checklist into a monthly routine check.

Common security pitfalls

  1. You suspect your account has been compromised

    Change your password immediately and ask your admin to check the account's recent activity; if necessary, disable the account first.

  2. A shared link has leaked

    Disable that link immediately, generate a new one (if you still need one), and assess how far the leak has spread.

  3. The browser keeps warning that the site is not secure

    Stop, and check the spelling of the URL and that it is https. If the warning persists, contact your admin; do not force your way through.

  4. You are not sure whether something should be public

    Default to "not public". When in doubt, close it, and open it when there is a real need.

Security FAQ

Are photos on a self-hosted server really safer than in the cloud?
Control of the data is yours, but so is the responsibility for security. The benefit of self-hosting is that your data never goes out to a third party, provided your accounts, passwords and shares are all kept under control.
Do I need two-factor authentication?
If Immich or the entry point supports it, we strongly recommend turning it on for admin accounts. It is the most effective step you can take for account security.
Does putting a password on a shared link help?
Yes, it adds a layer of protection. But the link is still a key, so set an expiry and limit the scope as well; do not rely on the password alone.
Can the Locked Folder protect my most sensitive photos?
It stops someone nearby from casually browsing through them, but it is not real file encryption. For truly sensitive content, do not rely on it alone.
How often should I run a security check?
We suggest a quick inventory once a month (accounts, shares, storage), and an immediate check whenever something looks off (such as a suspicious notification).
