Chapter 5

Timeline and browsing

Make sense of the time grouping on the main timeline (Timeline), and learn to open a photo, read its info, mark favorites and handle the basic browsing moves. By the end of this chapter, you'll be able to find and revisit photos comfortably, even among many thousands of them.

The timeline is where you'll spend most of your time

The Photos page is your photo timeline: photos are sorted by capture time, newest first, and grouped automatically by year and month. Learning to read it and move around it is the basic skill behind looking back on your photos and finding the one you want, day to day.

Chapter task: browse the timeline, open a photo, read the info panel, and mark a few photos as favorites. Everything here is read-only: you will not delete or change the photos themselves.

How the timeline is sorted and grouped

The timeline is not in upload order; it follows the capture time embedded in each photo (EXIF). Photos taken on nearby dates are automatically collected into one group, labeled with the month or the day, so you can jump to a period quickly.

Time grouping is adjustable: depending on the version, the timeline may have a month/day grouping density setting, which you can change on the screen or in the settings.

Five steps to browse your photos

  1. Stay on the Photos timeline

    Click Photos in the sidebar. You'll see the photo grid and its time groups.

  2. Scroll to a period

    Scroll down to the month you are after. With many photos, remembering the rough year makes it faster to land in the right place.

  3. Open a photo

    This takes you into the viewer's full-size view; use the left and right arrow keys to go to the previous or next photo.

  4. Read the info panel

    The viewer has an Info entry point, where you can see EXIF data such as capture time, location, camera and file size.

  5. Mark a few favorites

    In the viewer, or after selecting several photos, mark the ones you like as a favorite. Later, Favorites in the sidebar brings them all up at once.

Favorites and Archive

TrashHolds deleted photos for 30 days; you can restore them or empty itNot for 30 days; only then are they permanently deleted
Archive is not delete: Archive means "putting a photo away", not deleting it. When you cannot find a photo, look in Archive or Trash first, before you rush to upload it again.

Common browsing pitfalls

  1. The photo grid keeps spinning or is blank

    Refresh. With a large number of photos the first load is slower; once the thumbnails have been generated, browsing becomes smoother.

  2. One photo's thumbnail is blurry

    The thumbnail may still be generating; open the original and it is usually sharp. If it stays blurry, see the thumbnail troubleshooting in Chapter 12.

  3. A photo has "disappeared"

    Look in Archive, Trash and Favorites first, or use the search from Chapter 7. It may have been archived or deleted by mistake.

  4. The date on the timeline is wrong

    Usually the EXIF capture time is missing. You can edit and correct it in the info panel.

Browsing FAQ

Why did a photo end up on some day in the past?
The timeline is sorted by capture time. The capture date recorded in the photo's EXIF data decides where it appears; when it was uploaded has nothing to do with it.
What can the Map view do?
Photos with GPS location data are pinned on the map, so you can find photos by place. Photos without location data do not appear on the map.
What is the difference between favorites and albums?
Favorites is a quick list you build with a one-click mark; an album is a collection you organize, name and share by hand (Chapter 6). One photo can be in several albums at the same time.
Are photos in the trash deleted automatically?
Yes. By default they are kept for 30 days and then cleared automatically. If you deleted a photo by mistake, restore it from Trash before that deadline.
Can I download the original?
Yes. There is a download (Download) option in the viewer or in the menu, and it downloads the file at original quality.
