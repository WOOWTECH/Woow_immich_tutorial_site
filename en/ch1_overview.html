Chapter 1

Before you start: keep your memories at home

First, get clear on what Immich is, why you would self-host a photo library, and the account roles and public exposure you need to decide on before you start. By the end of this chapter, you'll know what this Immich setup can do for you, and which lines you must not cross.

Why keep your photos at home

The photo library on your phone is the biggest digital asset most people own: family photos, the kids growing up, travel records, easily tens of thousands of photos. Handing those memories to a cloud service is convenient, but a few things about it are unsettling: storage costs a monthly fee, your photos sit in someone else's data center, and when the service shuts down or changes its rules you have no choice but to go along.

Immich is a way to bring "the Google Photos experience" back into your own home. Your photos and videos live on your own server, and from a browser or the Immich mobile app you can browse, search, organize and share them just like a cloud photo service; the difference is that the data stays in your hands.

Chapter goal: not to teach you how to set up a server, but to make sure that before you start using it you understand what this Immich setup looks like, the account roles and the security boundaries. The hands-on work starts with the login in Chapter 2.
To be clear: half of the "security" of a self-hosted photo library is your own responsibility. If your account password is weak, or you hand out shared links carelessly, even the best setup will not stop it. This guide will keep coming back to "minimal exposure".

How to say it: Immich is pronounced roughly 'im-ich', with the ending like the German word for 'I' (ich). Do not overthink it; just say "Immich" as the docs spell it.

  • Entry point: nothing to install; open the URL in a browser and you can use it (Chapter 2).
  • Storage: "Storage space" in the bottom left shows used / total capacity, for example "61.3 GiB of 916.2 GiB used".

The architecture in one line: you (a browser or the mobile app) → Cloudflare Tunnel (an encrypted tunnel) → Immich on the WoowTech server. No public port has to be opened in between, and there is no IP address to remember.

Decide first: account roles and what is public

Before you start, two ideas decide how safe everything you do afterwards will feel: who has an account and what is public.

RoleWhat it can doWho it suits
AdminManage users, storage, background jobs and site-wide settingsWhoever maintains the server (usually WoowTech)
Regular userBack up, browse, organize and share their own photosFamily members, everyday use

For what is public there are also three common choices, from the safest to the most open:

  1. Nothing public

    Only people who log in with an account can see anything; even the entry point requires account authentication first. This is the default, and the safest way to use it.

  2. Share specific albums only

    Use the shared links from Chapter 8 to open one album to family and friends; nobody gets to see the rest of your photos.

  3. Wide open

    Expose the whole photo grid or the admin panel to the internet. This guide advises against it, unless you understand the risks very clearly.

Hard stop: do not make the admin panel, your password or the whole photo grid public just because it is convenient. Share only the one album the person needs to see, and revoke it afterwards.

Security boundaries before you start

Security in Immich is not "done once it is set up"; it is a few lines you have to hold every time you use it:

  • Account password: use a password that is long enough and not reused anywhere else; this goes double for the admin account. Never keep passwords in a notes app or a group chat.
  • HTTPS: the entry URL always starts with https://. If the browser warns "Not secure", stop; do not go on typing your credentials.
  • Shared links: a link you share is a key; whoever holds it can look. By default give it only to the people who need it, and control it with an expiry and permissions.
  • Minimal exposure: make public only the albums that need to be, never the whole site; do not hand admin features to people who have no need for them.
Rule of thumb: before any decision about what is public, ask one question first: "Could I live with a stranger seeing this?" If not, tighten it.

Before you start FAQ

How is Immich different from Google Photos?
The experience is very similar, but with Immich your photos and videos are stored on your own (or WoowTech's) server, not in Google's data centers. You control the data, but the responsibility for backups and maintenance is correspondingly on your side too.
Do I need to know how to code or set up a server?
Not at all, as a user. All you need is to be able to open a browser and use an app. WoowTech takes care of setup and maintenance; this guide only teaches you how to use it.
Will anyone else see my photos?
By default, only someone logged in with an account can see their own photos. Unless you create a shared link or make an album public yourself, nobody outside can see them. Just keep to the security checklist in Chapter 11 as you use it.
What if storage runs out?
First check the usage under "Storage space" in the bottom left, then tidy up (delete duplicates, archive, empty the trash). When you genuinely need more, contact the admin to expand storage; never delete files on the server yourself.
The entry URL will not open, or shows as not secure. What should I do?
First confirm the URL is https://your-immich.example (check for https and the full spelling). Note: replace this with the URL of your own Immich server (the one WoowTech gave you, or your own). If the browser shows a certificate warning, stop and contact the admin; do not skip past the warning and log in.
